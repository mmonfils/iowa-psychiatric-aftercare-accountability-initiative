### Reforming Iowa's Psychiatric Discharge System via Mandatory ASO Continuity of Care (HF 385)
**Why You Are Here:** Iowa hospitals routinely execute uncoordinated psychiatric discharges, shifting the systemic burden to families, homeless shelters, and jail cells. This blueprint empowers Iowa neighbors to organize, monitor, and enforce the statutory continuity-of-care guardrails established under House File 385.

---

## Situation
Vulnerable psychiatric patients across Iowa are routinely discharged from acute care facilities directly to the street under severe conditions of paranoia and clinical instability. This lack of basic coordination fractures critical connections with legal guardians, family networks, and localized mobile crisis teams. Left with insufficient medication supplies and zero outpatient handoffs, individuals face immediate risks of severe homelessness, systemic criminalization, and preventable deaths.
> *(Read full [Social Analysis](social-analysis.md))*

## Background
Iowa House File 385 addresses this systemic failure by establishing explicit discharge mandates for patients under involuntary court orders. The policy targets private hospital networks that utilize rapid bed turnover to lower administrative overhead, passing downstream friction costs onto municipal emergency services. True structural protection relies on leveraging Iowa's newly consolidated regional Administrative Service Organizations (ASOs) to enforce strict, auditable post-discharge care coordination.
> *(Read full [Policy Analysis](policy-analysis.md))*

## Assessment
The financial framework of the current status quo externalizes massive immediate operational costs onto county jails, public shelter networks, and emergency medical services. Furthermore, repeated cyclical hospitalizations cause severe, long-term human capital losses by triggering cumulative neurological degradation in untreated patients. Enforcing mandatory aftercare plans and guaranteed short-term medication supplies significantly mitigates these systemic public expenditures.
> *(Read full [Economic Analysis](economic-analysis.md))*

## Recommendation
Communities must organize to guarantee the full statutory implementation of HF 385 by requiring hospitals to execute explicit ASO care referrals and comprehensive suicide assessments before any discharge. Simultaneously, grassroots advocates must construct parallel, citizen-led data-monitoring systems to track post-hospitalization outcomes independently. This dual approach ensures immediate community-level protection while enforcing macro-level institutional transparency and legal compliance.
> *(Read full [Solution Analysis](solution-analysis.md))*

---

## Action
Advocates on the ground will deploy deep canvassing strategies to build deep neighborhood consensus and bypass sterile, bureaucratic policy jargon. Ground-level narratives must center on corporate hospital accountability, transparent public safety, and universal community values. By focusing directly on regional legislative targets and ASO leadership, community groups can effectively pressure institutions to execute the warm handoffs required to break the cycle of psychiatric streeting.

# Economic Analysis: Dual Cost Modeling

## 1. Executive Summary
The financial defense of the status quo rests on a flawed assessment of short-term hospital overhead. This dual-cost model demonstrates that uncoordinated psychiatric streeeting inflicts massive, long-term human capital losses and high operational friction costs on Iowa's public ledger. Investing in mandatory 7-day medication supplies and proactive case management dramatically reduces the aggregate municipal enforcement burden.

## 2. Human Capital Approach (Long-Term Value Losses)
* **Impacted Demographic / Workforce Segment:** Working-age Iowans experiencing severe neurodevelopmental or psychiatric crises (e.g., schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder) subject to repeated involuntary commitment cycles.
* **Estimated Lifetime/Long-Term Loss Per Unit:** Chronic, untreated cyclical psychosis accelerates irreversible neurological damage, permanently eroding cognitive baselines, workforce capacity, and long-term independent living potential.
* **Aggregate Community Valuation Impact:** Premature mortality, elevated suicide rates among uncoordinated discharge populations, and the permanent removal of uncompensated family caregivers from the active labor market to provide intensive safety-net supervision.

## 3. Friction Cost Approach (Immediate Operational Costs)
* **Frictional Waste Source A (Administrative & Emergency Overhead):** Hyper-inflated emergency department cycling costs, where single uncoordinated patients generate dozens of acute admissions annually due to rapid post-discharge decompensation.
* **Frictional Waste Source B (Operational Redundancy):** Local law enforcement expenditures via frequent wellness checks, crisis dispatches, and extended holds within county jail facilities lacking dedicated psychiatric infrastructure.

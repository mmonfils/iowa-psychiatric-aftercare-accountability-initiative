# Economic Analysis: Dual Cost Modeling

## 1. Executive Summary

The financial defense of the status quo rests on a flawed assessment of short term hospital overhead. This dual cost model demonstrates that uncoordinated psychiatric streeting inflicts massive, long term human capital losses and high operational friction costs on Iowa public ledgers. Investing in mandatory medication supplies and proactive case management dramatically reduces the aggregate municipal enforcement burden.

## 2. Human Capital Approach (Long Term Value Losses)

* **Impacted Demographic / Workforce Segment:** Working age Iowans experiencing severe neurodevelopmental or psychiatric crises (such as schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder) subject to repeated involuntary commitment cycles under Iowa Code Chapter 229.
* **Estimated Lifetime / Long Term Loss Per Unit:** Chronic, untreated cyclical psychosis accelerates irreversible neurological damage, permanently eroding cognitive baselines, workforce capacity, and long term independent living potential.
* **Aggregate Community Valuation Impact:** Premature mortality, elevated suicide rates among uncoordinated discharge populations, and the permanent removal of uncompensated family caregivers from the active labor market to provide intensive safety net supervision. The broader economic and programmatic variables are detailed in the [Iowa LSA Fiscal Note for HF 385](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/publications/FN/1524768.pdf).

## 3. Friction Cost Approach (Immediate Operational Costs)

* **Frictional Waste Source A (Administrative and Emergency Overhead):** Hyper inflated emergency department cycling costs, where single uncoordinated patients generate dozens of acute admissions annually due to rapid post discharge decompensation.
* **Frictional Waste Source B (Operational Redundancy):** Local law enforcement expenditures via frequent wellness checks, crisis dispatches, and extended holds within county jail facilities lacking dedicated psychiatric infrastructure. The practical systemic friction is documented in the [NASW Iowa Video Briefing on HF 385](https://youtu.be/RFmGSzbPNqY?si=pvH13jNiAKmj7hqw).
